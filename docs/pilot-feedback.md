# Video pilot: feedback from the first five families

The pilot answers one question: **will families use, and pay for, recorded isiNdebele lessons?** Show the Lesson 5 video to five families you already teach or know, then ask each parent the questions below.

Record every answer in the tracker (`records/hilys-tracker.xlsx`, **Pilot feedback** sheet). The **Course summary** sheet adds them up for you.

## Before you send the video

- [ ] Sija has checked the words in Lesson 5 (the recording kit lists them).
- [ ] The video plays on a phone, and the sound is clear without headphones.
- [ ] Add each family to the tracker's **Video course** sheet with the status **Pilot family**.
- [ ] Choose families with different ages and levels if you can, not only your keenest ones.

## 1. Message with the video

> Salibonani [name]! I'm making video lessons so children can learn isiNdebele between live lessons, or at their own pace.
>
> Could [child's name] try the first one? It's about 40 minutes and can be watched in two sittings: [video link]
>
> There's a worksheet to print as well: [worksheet link]
>
> Afterwards I'd love 5 minutes of your honest opinion. It will shape the whole course. Ngiyabonga!

## 2. Questions to ask (a few days later)

Ask on a call or a voice note if you can. Parents say more out loud than they type. If you send them on WhatsApp, send this:

> Thank you for trying the video lesson with [child's name]! Could you answer these 10 quick questions? Honest answers help most, even the uncomfortable ones.
>
> 1. How much of the video did [child's name] watch? (all / most / about half / less)
> 2. From 1 to 5, how interested did they stay? (1 = bored, 5 = loved it)
> 3. From 1 to 5, how clear were the sound and the picture?
> 4. Was the lesson too long, about right, or too short?
> 5. Did they join in: answering, repeating, clapping?
> 6. What was the best part?
> 7. What was hard or confusing?
> 8. If the whole course was like this, would you pay for it? (yes / maybe / no)
> 9. What would be a fair price per month?
> 10. Would you tell another family about it?

Two more things to ask in person, not in the message:

- **What did the child say or do afterwards?** A child who uses a word at dinner is better evidence than any score.
- **May we quote you on the website?** Only parents who say yes go in the reviews section (`reviews` in `public/assets/site.js`).

## 3. Reading the results

Five families is a small group, so look for patterns, not averages.

| What you see | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Most children watched most of it, and interest is 4 or more | The format works. Record the next lessons. |
| Children stopped around the same point | That part is too long or too hard. Shorten it before recording more. |
| "Too long" from 3 or more families | Split each lesson into two shorter videos. |
| Sound or picture below 4 | Fix the recording setup before anything else. |
| 3 or more say "yes" to paying | Open the waiting list to more families. |
| Mostly "maybe" | Ask what would make it a yes: a live class each month, a lower price, more lessons? |
| Fair price is well below the website price | Rethink the price, or what the plan includes, before launch. |

## 4. Afterwards

- Thank each family, and tell them one thing you changed because of what they said.
- Move families who want the course to **Waiting list** on the **Video course** sheet.
