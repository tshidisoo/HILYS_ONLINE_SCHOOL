# Intermediate lessons 2–4

These follow on from [Intermediate 1: Talking about me](intermediate-01-about-me.md). The rule stays the same: the child answers in **full isiNdebele sentences**, and you gently ask for it again with *Kutsho ngesiNdebele?* when they switch to English.

> **Check before teaching:** all isiNdebele here is a draft.

---

## Intermediate 2: My week

**Goal:** the child describes their week in sentences, with days and times.
**Culture:** Sunday in Zimbabwe, and what families do (church, visiting relatives, cooking together).

| Phrase | English |
|---|---|
| uMvulo … iSonto | Monday … Sunday |
| ekuseni | in the morning |
| emini | in the afternoon |
| ntambama | in the evening |
| ngolwesibili ngiya … | on Tuesday I go to … |
| ngemva kwesikolo | after school |

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Greeting |
| 3–8 | Review the questions from lesson 1, faster. |
| 8–18 | **My timetable.** Share a blank weekly grid on screen. The child tells you what they do each day, and you fill in the grid in isiNdebele as they speak. |
| 18–25 | **Compare.** Describe a typical week for a child in Bulawayo. The child spots 3 things that are the same and 3 that are different. |
| 25–28 | **Home task.** Tell a grandparent on the phone what you did this week. |
| 28–30 | Goodbye |

## Intermediate 3: At the market

**Goal:** the child buys and sells in a role-play, uses numbers above 10, and talks about prices in dollars.
**Culture:** markets in Zimbabwe and using US dollars there. Compare with pounds in the UK.

| Phrase | English |
|---|---|
| Ngicela … | Please may I have … |
| Yimalini? | How much is it? |
| Kuyadula! | It's expensive! |
| Kungabiza kancane? | Can it be a bit cheaper? |
| Nanku imali yakho | Here is your money |
| amadola | dollars |
| itshumi lanye … amatshumi amabili | 11 … 20 (check the forms you use) |

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Greeting |
| 3–10 | **Numbers 11–50.** Count in tens, then play "higher or lower". |
| 10–22 | **Market role-play.** The child sells first, with real items or pictures and prices. You bargain (*Kuyadula!*). Then swap. Bargaining makes children laugh and talk. |
| 22–26 | **Culture.** Share photos of a Zimbabwean market and talk about bargaining. |
| 26–28 | **Home task.** Help a parent with the shopping list in isiNdebele. |
| 28–30 | Goodbye |

## Intermediate 4: Telling a story in order

**Goal:** the child retells a folktale in isiNdebele using linking words.
**Culture:** the folktale itself, and the *Kwasukasukela… Cosi!* tradition.

| Linking word | English |
|---|---|
| Kuqala | First |
| Besekusithi | Then |
| Ngemva kwalokho | After that |
| Ekucineni | Finally |
| Ngoba | Because |

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Greeting |
| 3–10 | **Tell the story.** Tell a folktale fully in isiNdebele, with pictures. |
| 10–14 | **Check.** Ask *Ngubani…? Kwenzakalani…? Ngoba…?* (Who? What happened? Why?) |
| 14–24 | **Retell.** Put 5 pictures on screen. The child retells the story using the linking words. Write down their sentences. |
| 24–28 | **Moral.** Ask *Indaba le isifundisani?* (What does this story teach us?) |
| 28–30 | Goodbye. Send the parent the written retelling as a progress note. |

## After lesson 4
Intermediate children usually need 2–3 months before moving up to Conversation. Moving up makes sense when they can retell a story with few English words.
