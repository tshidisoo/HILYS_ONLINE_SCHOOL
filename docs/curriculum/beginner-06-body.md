# Beginner 6: My body

**Goal:** the child can name 8 parts of the body and follow simple instructions.
**Culture:** an action song (a body-parts song you know, or "Simon says" as *uSija uthi…*).
**Flashcards:** set *Lesson 6*.

> Draft isiNdebele: check spellings before teaching.

## Review (from Lesson 5)
Play "Who am I?" with 4 animals.

## New words

| isiNdebele | English |
|---|---|
| ikhanda | head |
| amehlo | eyes |
| indlebe | ear |
| ikhala | nose |
| umlomo | mouth |
| isandla | hand |
| unyawo | foot |
| amadolo | knees |

**Instructions:** *Thinta…* (Touch…), *Phakamisa…* (Lift…), *Ima!* (Stop!)

## Lesson flow

1. **Greeting and review (6 min).**
2. **New words (8 min).** Touch each part as you say it, and the child copies. Then go faster.
3. **Game: *uSija uthi…* (5 min).** Like "Simon says". If you say *uSija uthi thinta ikhanda!*, the child touches their head. If you don't say *uSija uthi* first, they shouldn't move. Ask the parent to play too.
4. **Song (6 min).** Sing an action song that names body parts. Start slowly, then speed up.
5. **Home task (3 min).** Play *uMama uthi…* with a parent at home.
6. **Goodbye (2 min).** *Sala kuhle!*

## Why this works on Zoom
Movement games keep 6-year-olds focused for the whole lesson, and the parent can see learning happen.
