# Intermediate 1: Talking about me

**For:** children who understand some isiNdebele at home but answer in English.
**Goal:** the child answers in **full isiNdebele sentences** about themselves, not single words or English.
**Culture:** praise names and clan names (*izibongo*). The child learns their own family's *isibongo*.

> Draft isiNdebele: check every phrase before teaching.

## The main rule for Intermediate
These children usually *can* answer in isiNdebele but are used to switching to English. Gently ask for it again in isiNdebele every time: *Kutsho ngesiNdebele?* ("Say it in isiNdebele?"). Make it a game, not a correction.

## Before the lesson
Ask the parent for the family's *isibongo* (clan name) and, if they know it, a line or two of its praises. The child will learn them in this lesson.

## Sentence patterns

| Question | Answer pattern |
|---|---|
| *Ibizo lakho ngubani?* | *Ibizo lami ngu…* |
| *Ulemnyaka emingaki?* | *Ngilemnyaka e…* |
| *Uhlala ngaphi?* | *Ngihlala e…* (London, Leeds…) |
| *Uthanda ukwenzani?* | *Ngithanda uku…* (dlala, bala, dansa) |
| *Isibongo sakho ngubani?* | *Isibongo sami ngu…* |

## Lesson flow

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Greeting, in isiNdebele only from the first word. |
| 3–12 | **Interview.** Ask the 5 questions. The child answers in full sentences. Write their answers on screen as they speak. |
| 12–15 | **Swap.** The child interviews you with the same questions. |
| 15–25 | **Izibongo.** Explain that every Ndebele family has a clan name and praises that tell where the family comes from. Teach the child their own *isibongo* and practise saying the praises proudly. |
| 25–28 | **Home task.** Ask a grandparent on the phone about the family *isibongo* and one story about it. |
| 28–30 | Goodbye. |
