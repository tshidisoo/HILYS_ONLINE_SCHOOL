# Beginner 2: My family

**Goal:** the child can name 6 family members and say who lives in their home.
**Culture:** folktale, *uNogwaja and the animals*.
**Flashcards:** set *Lesson 2*.

> Draft isiNdebele: check spellings and family terms before teaching. Ndebele kinship words carry more detail than English ones, so choose the forms you want children to learn first.

## Review (from Lesson 1)
*Salibonani*, *Unjani?*, *Ngiyaphila*, *Ibizo lami ngu…*

## New words

| isiNdebele | English |
|---|---|
| umama | mother |
| ubaba | father |
| ugogo | grandmother |
| ubabamkhulu | grandfather |
| umntwana | child |
| usisi | sister / older girl |
| ubhudi | brother / older boy |
| umndeni | family |

**Sentence pattern:** *Lo ngumama.* (This is mum.) *Lo ngugogo.* (This is grandma.)

## Lesson flow

1. **Greeting and review (6 min).** Show Lesson 1 flashcards quickly and see what the child remembers.
2. **New words (10 min).** Show the family cards. Then ask the parent to stand behind the child and say *Lo ngumama!* Ask the child to point to people in their home, or in a photo, and name them.
3. **Folktale (9 min).** Tell a short uNogwaja (hare) story.
   - Start with the traditional opening: you say *Kwasukasukela…* and the child answers *Cosi!* Practise this twice first. Children love the call and response.
   - Keep it to 3–4 scenes with pictures on screen.
   - Use family words inside the story (e.g. the hare's mother, the lion's children).
   - End by asking: *Ngubani owayehlakaniphile?* (Who was clever?)
4. **Home task (3 min).** Draw your family and write each person's isiNdebele name underneath with a parent's help. Show the drawing next lesson.
5. **Goodbye (2 min).** *Sala kuhle!*

## Note for you
Pick an uNogwaja story you know well and tell it in your own words. Use mostly English for Beginner level, but keep the opening, the animal names and the family words in isiNdebele. Use more isiNdebele as the weeks go on.
