# Conversation 1: My news

**For:** children who already speak isiNdebele and need to keep it strong.
**Goal:** the child talks for 2–3 minutes in isiNdebele without switching to English, and learns words for things they usually say in English.
**Culture:** proverbs (*izaga*), learning one a week.

> Draft isiNdebele: check every phrase and proverb before teaching.

## The main idea
Children who speak isiNdebele often have "home" words but not "school" or "UK life" words, so they switch to English for those. In Conversation lessons, the child does most of the talking. Your job is to collect the English words they reach for and give them the isiNdebele.

## Lesson flow

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Greeting and small talk. |
| 3–12 | **My news.** The child tells you about their week: school, friends, sport, a birthday. Ask follow-up questions only. Each time they use an English word, write it down quietly. |
| 12–17 | **Word catch.** Show the English words you collected and give the isiNdebele for each. The child uses each one in a new sentence. |
| 17–25 | **Proverb of the week.** Teach one *isaga*, what it means and when you'd use it. Then the child tells a short story from their own life that fits the proverb. |
| 25–28 | **Home task.** Use the proverb at home this week when it fits, and tell a parent what it means. |
| 28–30 | Goodbye. |

## Proverbs to start with
Choose 4 proverbs you like and know well. Good ones for children are about teamwork, patience, or respect for elders. Add them here once you've chosen:

## Keep a "word catch" list
Keep a running list of each child's caught words in the tracker's Notes, or in a separate file. These words are their next lesson's review, and a good thing to show parents in the monthly progress note.
